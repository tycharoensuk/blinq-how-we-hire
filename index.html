Engineering

The Engineering interview process

A technical but human process designed to evaluate your engineering thinking, communication, and how you approach real problems.

What to expect — stage by stage
1
Stage 1
Recruiter Screen
A conversation with our Talent team to get to know you — what motivates you, your background, and what you're looking for next. We'll also tell you more about Blinq and the role.
2
Stage 2
Hiring Manager Interview
A deeper conversation with the hiring manager assessing your technical skills, experience, and culture fit. Expect questions that dig into how you think and how you work.
3
Stage 3
Onsite Interviews
Your onsite consists of up to three sessions. We'll progress you through each step based on how the previous one goes.
3a — Technical Interview
A panel interview covering engineering craft and a coding challenge. We want to see how you think through problems, structure your code, and communicate your approach. If this goes well, we'll move you forward — if not, we'll wrap up here.
3b — Lunch with the Team
An informal chance to meet the broader engineering team over lunch. Lunch is on us — come as you are and get a genuine feel for the culture and the people you'd be working with.
3c — Systems Design
You'll be given a problem statement with a set of requirements and asked to design a high-level solution. The goal is to evaluate your technical thinking, clarity, and communication — not to write production-ready code.
How to prepare

Interview focus areas

Stage 2 — Hiring Manager Interview

We assess you across five IC competencies. Here's what we're looking for at the hiring manager stage:

Competency What it looks like
Technical Excellence Language and platform fluency, code quality instincts, debugging methodology, testing practices, framework depth
Architecture & Systems Design patterns, modularisation strategy, scalability thinking, data modelling, system decomposition
Better on the Inside CI/CD, build tooling, DX improvements, testing infrastructure, process automation, observability
Teamwork Cross-team collaboration, code review culture, documentation habits, consensus-building on design decisions
Leadership Mentoring senior ICs, org-level influence, hiring bar ownership, platform strategy, long-term technical vision

Stage 3 — Onsite Interviews

3a — Technical Interview
  • Write clean code with clear naming conventions and logical structure
  • Walk through your thought process out loud — interviewers want to see how you think, not just what you produce
  • Be ready to talk through how you approach edge cases and error handling
  • We use AI at Blinq, so feel free to showcase how you use AI tools in your coding workflow
3c — Systems Design

We recommend using a whiteboarding tool to structure your thinking. Pick whichever you're most comfortable with:

Excalidraw diagrams.net Miro
  • Start by sketching a rough wireframe of how data will be displayed in the UI — let that inform your systems design
  • Put down your first thought and iterate. We care more about your thought process and the tradeoffs you're making than a perfect design
  • Embrace higher levels of abstraction early — it's fine to say "we need a database here" before deciding what type. Deep dives can come later
  • Don't let early decisions unnecessarily close off options later in the design
  • Feel free to make assumptions — just state them as you go
Sales

The Sales interview process

We're building a cross-border, multi-city sales team. We want people with grit, tenacity, and hustle — who love to sell and want to help shape where this thing goes next.

What to expect — stage by stage
1
Stage 1
Recruiter Screen
A conversation with our Talent team to get to know you — what motivates you, your background, and what you're looking for next. We'll also tell you more about Blinq and the role.
2
Stage 2
Hiring Manager Interview
A deeper conversation with the hiring manager digging into your sales experience. Expect behavioural and situational questions that explore how you've approached deals, managed pipeline, and performed under pressure.
3
Stage 3
Deep Dive
A two-part final stage designed to assess both your strategic thinking and your ability to sell in the room. You'll complete a take-home case study task, followed by a live role play session where we put you in a realistic sales scenario.
What we're looking for
  • High-energy communicators who thrive on outreach and resilience
  • Curious, coachable, and eager to grow into closing roles
  • Takes initiative to own daily output and pipeline
  • Comfortable working independently across time zones
  • Resourceful, organized, and data-driven in how they prospect
How to prepare
  • Understand Blinq's mission, product, and how we solve problems
  • Be ready to talk through your outreach strategy (email, call, LinkedIn)
  • Have examples of how you've handled rejection and stayed motivated
  • Bring metrics: meetings booked, call-to-meeting conversion rates
  • Think about how you prioritize leads and personalize outreach at scale
Practice Questions

We're looking for specific examples, not theory.

Tell me about a cold prospect you turned into a meeting — how did you do it?
How do you prioritize your day when you're juggling 100+ accounts?
What tools or tactics have helped you stand out in outbound?
What's one mistake you've made in outreach — and what did you learn?
How do you stay accountable when your manager is asleep (time zones)?
Product Management

The Product Management interview process

We're looking for product people who can be both strategic and scrappy — who move fast, learn from signals, and build trust through data-driven storytelling.

What to expect — stage by stage
1
Stage 1
Recruiter Screen
A conversation with our Talent team covering your background, motivations, and cultural alignment. We'll tell you more about the role, the team, and what to expect next.
2
Stage 2
Hiring Manager Interview
A deeper dive into your experience, thinking, and approach. Expect questions about product decisions you've made, tradeoffs you've navigated, and how you've driven outcomes.
3
Stage 3
Deep Dive
A two-part session with the broader product team:
3a — Live Problem Challenge
We'll present you with a real problem live and work through it together with the team. This is less about a perfect answer and more about how you think, structure ambiguity, and collaborate in real time.
3b — Take-Home & Panel Presentation
Following the live challenge, you'll receive a short take-home task to work on in your own time. You'll then present your thinking back to a panel — we're looking for clarity, structure, and how you communicate complex ideas.
4
Stage 4
Culture Interview
A conversation focused on values alignment and mutual fit. We want to understand how you work with others, what drives you, and whether Blinq is the right environment for you to do your best work. Expect questions around how you've handled ambiguity, navigated disagreement, given and received feedback, and what kind of team culture you thrive in. This is also your chance to ask us anything — about the team, the direction of the product, or what it's really like to work at Blinq.
Experience we're looking for
  • End-to-end product ownership, ideally in a B2C & B2B SaaS environment
  • Experience moving products from 0 to 1
  • Aligning with leadership, writing specs, joining customer calls
  • Writing clearly and presenting insights with structure
  • Bias for speed, learning, and ownership
Mindset we value
  • Understands tradeoffs between speed and building for scale
  • Comfortable being both strategic and scrappy
  • Defines scope quickly and validates fast
  • Builds trust through data-driven storytelling
  • Balances customer empathy with GTM considerations
How to prepare
1
Know Blinq
Review our product, target users, and market. Consider how you'd measure product success at our current stage of growth.
2
Be Specific
We'll dig into past work so come with 1–2 case studies. We're less interested in theory — more in real examples of decisions, teamwork, and outcomes.
3
Bring a Data Lens
Think through how you've used data in the past. Share experience of partnering with data teams or conducting your own analysis to drive decisions.

Interview focus areas

Business Operations

The Business Operations interview process

What to expect — stage by stage

Interview focus areas

  • Be honest about what you'd want to validate further or what data you didn't have — intellectual honesty is valued here
  • Anticipate questions and be ready to defend your conclusions while staying open to challenge
  • If you used AI tools to assist your analysis or workflow, feel free to share how — we embrace that at Blinq

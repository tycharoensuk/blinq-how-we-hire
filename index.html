Our hiring philosophy

Engineering

The Engineering interview process

Technical rigor matters here. So does how you think out loud, communicate trade-offs, and show up as a teammate. This process gives you space to do all three.

What to expect, stage by stage
1
Stage 1
Recruiter chat
Get to know Blinq and the opportunity ahead. We'll learn more about your background, motivations, and what you're looking for next, while giving you a clear picture of our team, culture, and mission.
2
Stage 2
Hiring manager interview
A deeper conversation with your future manager to explore your experience, strengths, and approach to work. We'll dive into your accomplishments, how you think, and the impact you've created throughout your career.
3
Stage 3
Onsite interviews
Your onsite is up to three sessions. We progress you through each one based on how the previous went.
3a — Technical interview
A panel interview covering engineering craft and a coding challenge. We want to see how you think through problems, structure your code, and communicate your approach. If this goes well, we move you forward. If not, we'll wrap up here.
3b — Lunch with the team
An informal chance to meet the broader engineering team over lunch. Lunch is on us. Come as you are and get a genuine feel for the culture and the people you'd be working alongside.
3c — Systems design
You'll be given a problem statement with requirements and asked to design a high-level solution. We're evaluating your technical thinking, clarity, and communication. This isn't about writing production-ready code.
How to prepare

Interview focus areas

Stage 2 — Hiring manager interview

We assess you across five IC competencies. Here's what we're looking for in the hiring manager conversation:

Competency What it looks like
Technical Excellence Language and platform fluency, code quality instincts, debugging methodology, testing practices, framework depth
Architecture & Systems Design patterns, modularisation strategy, scalability thinking, data modelling, system decomposition
Better on the Inside CI/CD, build tooling, DX improvements, testing infrastructure, process automation, observability
Teamwork Cross-team collaboration, code review culture, documentation habits, consensus-building on design decisions
Leadership Mentoring senior ICs, org-level influence, hiring bar ownership, platform strategy, long-term technical vision

Stage 3 — Onsite interviews

3a — Technical interview
  • Write clean code with clear naming conventions and logical structure
  • Walk through your thought process out loud. Interviewers want to see how you think, not just what you produce
  • Be ready to talk through how you approach edge cases and error handling
  • We use AI at Blinq, so feel free to show how you use AI tools in your coding workflow
3c — Systems Design

We recommend using a whiteboarding tool to structure your thinking. Pick whichever you're most comfortable with:

Excalidraw diagrams.net Miro
  • Start by sketching a rough wireframe of how data will be displayed in the UI. Let that inform your systems design
  • Put down your first thought and iterate. We care more about your thought process and the trade-offs you're making than a perfect design
  • Embrace higher levels of abstraction early. It's fine to say "we need a database here" before deciding what type. Deep dives can come later
  • Don't let early decisions unnecessarily close off options later in the design
  • Make assumptions freely, just state them as you go

The Sales interview process

We're building a cross-border, multi-city sales team. If you love to sell and want a real say in where this thing goes next, you're going to fit right in.

Practice Questions

We're looking for specific examples, not theory.

Tell me about a cold prospect you turned into a meeting. How did you do it?
How do you prioritize your day when you're juggling 100+ accounts?
What tools or tactics have helped you stand out in outbound?
What's one mistake you've made in outreach and what did you learn from it?
How do you stay accountable when your manager is in a different time zone?
Product Management

The Product Management interview process

We're looking for product people who can be both strategic and scrappy. People who move fast, learn from real signals, and know how to bring others along with data and clarity.

What to expect, stage by stage

3b — Take-home & panel presentation

  • Aligning with leadership, writing specs, joining customer calls
  • Writing clearly and presenting insights with structure
  • A genuine bias for speed, learning, and ownership
Mindset we value
  • Understands trade-offs between speed and building for scale
  • Comfortable being both strategic and scrappy
  • Defines scope quickly and validates fast
  • Builds trust through clear, data-informed storytelling
  • Balances customer empathy with GTM considerations
How to prepare
1
Know Blinq
Use the product. Review our target users and the market. Think about how you'd measure product success at our current stage.
2
Be specific
We'll dig into past work so come with 1-2 case studies. We're less interested in theory and more interested in real decisions, real teamwork, and real outcomes.
3
Bring a data lens
Think through how you've used data to drive decisions. Share experience partnering with data teams or running your own analysis.
